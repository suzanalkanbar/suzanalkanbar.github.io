NS Dutch Railways · Smartwatch · Accessibility

An announcement you can feel

A smartwatch app that turns station announcements into vibrations and a clear alert on the wrist, for travellers who can’t hear the announcement.

  • Role: UX/UI & Interaction Designer
  • Duration: 3 months
  • Team: Solo project
  • Platform: watchOS
  • Tools: Figma, ProtoPie

Introduction

When a train changes platform, it is announced over the speakers. Deaf and hard-of-hearing travellers miss that message, and often the train with it. I designed a watch app that replaces the announcement with something you can feel and see.

The brief came from school: NS (Dutch Railways) doesn’t have a smartwatch touchpoint yet, and the question was how a watch could help travellers at the station. I chose to focus on deaf and hard-of-hearing travellers, because they miss the most when something changes.

Problem

Announcements are audio only. Departure boards have to be watched constantly, and NS notifications arrive on a phone in your pocket.

Users

Deaf and hard-of-hearing train travellers, on a busy platform, under time pressure.

My role

Solo project: research, concept, interaction design and the complete watch design in Figma and ProtoPie, built with the NS design system. The phone screens are the existing NS app, lightly adapted.

Design question

How can NS inform deaf and hard-of-hearing travellers in time about platform changes and delays at the station, without them depending on sound?

What goes wrong?

Updates often come only through announcements. Deaf travellers miss platform changes and end up following the crowd, which often takes them to the wrong place.

03 · Alerting without sound

How do existing designs do it?

Alarm clocks, doorbells and smoke alarms for deaf people combine light and vibration. You feel the vibration even when you’re not looking, and you see the light even if you miss the vibration.

Source ↗

Why a smartwatch? It is always on the wrist, and it reaches the traveller without sound and without them having to look for anything.
Design decisions

Designing for feeling and seeing

Feel before you read

Every change starts with a vibration that gets stronger as long as the traveller doesn’t respond.

Yellow is calm, red is action

The border shows the status without words: yellow when all is well, red until a change is confirmed.

Two words first

First only “⚠ Track change”. If the traveller keeps looking, the new track appears (watchOS: short look → long look).

Black with NS colours

Black for maximum contrast and battery life, with colours from the NS design system.

Sketch wide first, then choose

For each core interaction I first came up with and sketched four directions on paper. I weighed each one against interaction theory before working out one choice in Figma.

Phone + watch

The phone plans, the watch warns

The watch picks up the journey from the NS app automatically, so the traveller doesn’t have to do anything extra at the station. One tap on “Akkoord” (OK) confirms which journey is followed.

The core flow

Fatima on platform 4

Fatima is deaf. She is waiting for the 07:56 intercity and looking at her phone, not at the departure boards.

  1. 07:52:00The platform changes to 7. She feels a vibration and sees the red border.
  2. 07:52:02Short look: “⚠ Track change”.
  3. 07:52:04Long look: “Track 7” with an animated arrow. She swipes.
  4. 07:52:05Confirmed. Fatima catches her train.

What if she doesn’t respond?

There is no announcement as a safety net. So the vibration gets stronger every 5 seconds until the change is confirmed.

MomentVisualHaptics (watchOS)
Phase 1 · changeRed border flashesMedium, every 0.75 s
Phase 2 · no responseRed border pulsesHeavy, every 0.5 s
Phase 3 · still nothingRed border blinks fastFailure, every 0.25 s
ConfirmedGreen check markSuccess
Why a swipe and not a button? A swipe covers the whole screen, so fewer mis-taps under time pressure. It is the standard watchOS gesture, and the animated arrow makes it visible.
States

Every situation gets its own screen

When a train is cancelled or a connection is missed, the watch hands off to the phone, where there is room for alternatives. An error vibrates more softly on purpose, so you can feel it isn’t a platform change.

Made for a 2-second glance

  • The track number is the largest: it is the only thing the traveller really needs.
  • Touch targets of at least 44 pt. The OK button spans the full width.
  • No more than two actions per screen.
  • Journey and Status are two tabs, so each screen answers one question: “where am I going?” or “what’s happening now?”. That way a change (4 → 7) fills the screen, without the route around it.
  • 198 × 242 pt canvas (Apple Watch 45 mm).
